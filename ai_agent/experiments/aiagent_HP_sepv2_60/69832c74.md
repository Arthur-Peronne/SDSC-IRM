## Training Dynamics
Key mechanistic detail missed in the hypothesis: `patience_scheduler` defaults to
`patience // 5`, so patience=45 also changed the LR-decay scheduler's patience from 6
to 9 epochs — it did not just extend the tail, it stretched the ENTIRE schedule. lr
stayed at 6e-4 until epoch 51 here (vs epoch 42 in the champion, patience_scheduler=6)
giving 9 extra epochs of exploration at the productive high lr before the first
decay, then longer windows at each subsequent rung too. Best epoch shifted from 45 to
65, and the best val loss reached (0.000541) is the lowest of the entire campaign.
Early stopped at epoch 110 (vs 75) — a longer run overall, but not "wasted tail
epochs": the extra epochs were spent productively across the whole decay schedule,
not just idling at the end.

## Conclusion
Hypothesis wrong, informatively: I predicted neutral-to-negative because I only
considered patience as "how long to wait after the last improvement," missing that
`patience_scheduler: null` couples it to the LR decay cadence too. avg_validation_R2_mean
improved 0.8000 -> 0.8089 (+0.0090), the fourth CHAMPION combining the two winning
axes found so far (lr=6e-4, noise_std=0.0001) with the longer schedule. Train R2 also
rose further (0.8668, the highest yet) while val R2 improved too — both moved together
here, unlike the regularization trials where val gains (if any) came with flat-or-lower
train. This is consistent with the mechanism: a longer effective schedule let the
model reach a genuinely better optimum, not just fit noise longer. Given the clear
win, patience is not exhausted as an axis — test pushing it further (e.g. 60) to see
if the trend continues, now understanding the real lever is the coupled
patience/patience_scheduler schedule length, not idle waiting.
