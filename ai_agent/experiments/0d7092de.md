## Hypothesis
dropout_rate=0.05 failed badly and consistently (twice, under two different
schedules), always by cutting train R2 far more than it helped val R2. But only 0.05
was ever tested — a much smaller dose (0.01) has a qualitatively different effect:
at low rates, dropout approximates a mild noise-injection closer in spirit to
noise_std (which helped) than to a real capacity cut. I predict this is more likely
to be near-neutral (like weight_decay) than to reproduce 0.05's large failure,
though given the established ~0.03-0.04 noise floor, distinguishing "near-neutral"
from "small genuine win" will likely not be possible from a single run — the main
question this answers is whether dropout is harmful at ANY nonzero rate, or only
above some threshold, completing the dropout axis's characterization.

## Implementation
Single-field change in configs/autoencoder.yaml: dropout_rate 0.0 -> 0.01, on top of
the champion's lr=6e-4, noise_std=0.0001, patience=60. weight_decay=0 unchanged. No
architecture change.

than dropout=0.05's worst result (-0.0410 to -0.0151 depending on schedule) is NOT
what happened — 0.01 landed WORSE in this single draw than some 0.05 results, which
is counter to the "5x gentler dose, 5x smaller cost" expectation. However, given the
campaign's established ~0.03-0.04 single-run noise floor, this result cannot be
cleanly separated from "just an unlucky draw at a genuinely mildly-negative or
near-neutral rate" — train R2 did NOT collapse the way it did at 0.05, consistent
with dropout=0.01 being a much gentler capacity tax mechanistically, but the val
outcome this one run happened to land poorly. Directionally, dropout still does not
show any positive signal at any tested rate (0.0 champion > 0.01 > 0.05), which is
enough to conclude the dropout axis offers no upside at latent_dim=60 regardless of
magnitude — confirming rather than qualifying the earlier rejection. Champion remains
bed745a0. The dropout axis is now closed at every tested granularity.
