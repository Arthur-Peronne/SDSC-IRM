## Hypothesis
This is the root trial of a new campaign at latent_dimensions=60 (previous campaign
optimized the same architecture at latent_dimensions=240). Rather than carry over the
dim=240 champion's HPs (lr=8e-4, dropout_rate=0.05), I start from neutral/near-zero
values (lr=1e-5, weight_decay=0, dropout_rate=0, noise_std=0, patience=30) because a
4x smaller latent bottleneck changes the optimization landscape: less capacity to
overfit means the regularization (dropout, weight_decay, noise) that helped at dim=240
may not be needed or may even hurt convergence at dim=60, and the learning rate that
was optimal for a wider/deeper effective path at dim=240 is not assumed to transfer.
This baseline establishes the reference avg_validation_R2_mean against which
subsequent HP trials at this latent_dim are judged.

## Implementation
No code change. configs/autoencoder.yaml already has latent_dimensions=60 and the 5
opened hyperparameters at: lr=1e-5, weight_decay=0.0, dropout_rate=0.0, noise_std=0.0,
patience=30. n_epochs=200 (early stopping via patience, n_val=20>0, compute_metrics=true).

## Training Dynamics
Very smooth, monotonic descent from epoch 1 (val 0.2451) to the best epoch 52 (val
0.000927) — no spikes, no NaNs, no sign of instability, consistent with the
conservative lr=1e-5. The plateau-scheduler (patience_scheduler = patience//5 = 6)
started halving lr around epoch 58-59 and had crushed it to 6.25e-07 by epoch 80,
i.e. training was effectively frozen well before the early-stopping counter (patience
30) expired at epoch 82 — the last ~25 epochs contributed nothing. Around the best
epoch the val curve is noisy at fixed lr (e.g. epoch 47: 0.000988, 48: 0.001052, 49:
0.000946 "best", 50: 0.000994) — batch_size=1 noise, not divergence. Train loss was
still ticking down slowly at the end while val had stopped improving 30 epochs
earlier: a train/val gap (R2 0.8347 vs 0.6517) that is real but not runaway.

## Conclusion
Baseline established: avg_validation_R2_mean=0.6517 at latent_dim=60, well below the
dim=240 baseline (0.8075) — expected, a 4x smaller bottleneck retains less
information and reconstruction is a strictly harder problem. The hypothesis behind
starting from neutral HPs (less capacity -> less need for regularization) is not
contradicted: even with dropout=weight_decay=noise=0, the train/val gap is moderate
(0.83 vs 0.65), not the runaway overfitting that would indicate regularization is
urgently needed. Two more informative observations for the next trials: (1) lr=1e-5
is very conservative — the scheduler had to decay it 4x before the patience counter
ran out, and the model was still improving at a crawl each epoch pre-plateau, so a
higher lr is a natural, cheap first axis to test (faster/cleaner convergence to a
possibly better optimum, mirroring the dim=240 campaign where raising lr from the
smoke-test baseline was eventually part of the winning combination); (2) the high val
R2 std (0.2155) signals uneven per-patient reconstruction quality worth keeping an
eye on across trials, though not actionable via the 5 opened HPs directly.
