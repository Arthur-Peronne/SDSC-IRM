## Conclusion
Hypothesis did not hold, and it failed in an interesting way: `validation_R2_mean` actually improved
to 0.7361, the **best reconstruction fidelity of the whole campaign** (beating even the champion's
0.7321) — so weight_decay=1e-5/dropout=0.1 genuinely helped generalization on the reconstruction
task, as regularization normally would. But `classification_accuracy_val` dropped to 0.658333
(-0.042 vs champion, FAILURE), with per-seed accuracy 0.75/0.675/0.55 — a *wider* spread than the
champion's 0.725/0.65/0.725, the opposite of my prediction that regularization would reduce
variance.

This falsifies the specific "champion is regularization-starved" reading of the DeepFC failure: it
is not simply that more regularization helps recover lost ground. Combined with the strided-pooling
trial (2dd4cd5f, R2 unchanged but accuracy down), this is now the *second* trial this campaign where
reconstruction quality and classification accuracy move in opposite directions. Mechanistic read:
the network is trained on reconstruction MSE alone; dropout at the bottleneck randomly zeroes latent
dimensions during training, which pushes the encoder toward a more redundant/averaged-out encoding
that is robust to that noise and reconstructs well on average — but a downstream fixed-weight
logistic regression may rely on precise, non-redundant per-dimension structure that this kind of
regularization blurs. Reconstruction generalization and classification-latent quality are not the
same target in this pipeline, and interventions that classically help one can hurt the other.
