# Trial 25eae42a — AE3dAsymDilatedAttention — FAILURE

## Hypothesis
Four consecutive trials (b606a10f, 0cadad28, 001b5a08, e4e99f58) have failed to improve classification_accuracy_val by manipulating channel width (×2, ×0.5, encoder-only) or weight_decay (100×). This pattern strongly suggests that capacity and L2 regularization are not the levers that can improve this metric — the champion's architecture is already well-tuned for its current design.

This trial proposes a fundamentally different encoder mechanism: **dilated convolutions with Squeeze-and-Excitation attention**. Dilated convolutions increase the receptive field exponentially (1→2→4) without losing spatial resolution, allowing the network to capture multi-scale cardiac context — from local tissue textures to global heart geometry — in a single forward pass. SE attention performs channel-wise feature recalibration, prioritizing features that encode cardiac structures over background noise.

The key mechanistic prediction is that the latent representation will be more discriminative for the classification task because:
1. Multi-scale context provides richer structural information than single-scale convolutions
2. Attention focuses the bottleneck on diagnostically relevant features
3. The combination captures both "what" (cardiac structures) and "where" (spatial relationships)

I predict classification_accuracy_val will exceed the champion's 0.6083.

## Conclusion
Hypothesis **rejected**: classification_accuracy_val=0.4917 (-0.1167 vs champion), a substantial drop. The dilated attention encoder produces a stable, well-converging model (val loss ~0.003, even better than champion's ~0.003-0.004) but the latent representation is less discriminative for the downstream classification task. validation_R2_mean=0.635 is also lower than champion's 0.682, suggesting the dilated convolutions don't reconstruct as faithfully despite the larger receptive field.

**Mechanistic explanation**: The dilated convolutions with increasing dilation (1→2→4) create a large effective receptive field, but this may be a double-edged sword. At dilation=4, the effective kernel is 9×9×9, and with two sequential convolutions per block, the effective RF per block is ~17×17×17. On feature maps of size (32,8,16,16) at enc3, this means the receptive field covers the entire spatial extent — the network is essentially seeing the whole heart at every position. This global context is valuable for reconstruction but may *blur* the fine-grained spatial relationships that the classifier needs to discriminate between patient groups. The SE attention, while theoretically beneficial, operates at the channel level and cannot recover spatial specificity that has been lost to the large receptive field.

In contrast, the champion's ResSeparableConv3DBlock uses standard 3×3×3 convolutions (effective RF=7×7×7 with two layers), which preserve more local spatial structure. The residual connections further help the network learn fine-grained features by providing a direct gradient path.

**Key insight**: A larger receptive field does not automatically translate to better downstream performance. For this classification task, the spatial precision of the latent representation matters more than the breadth of context. The champion's narrower receptive field with residual connections happens to preserve the right kind of information.

**Next direction**: The dilated attention idea is not entirely dead — it may need a smaller dilation schedule (e.g., 1→1→1 or 1→2→2) to preserve more local structure, or it may need to be combined with residual connections to stabilize training. But as implemented, the 1→2→4 dilation schedule is too aggressive for this task. I will pivot to a different direction.
